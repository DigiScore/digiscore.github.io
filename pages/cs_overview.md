### Musicians employed: 
Approximately 60–70 musicians, including composers and performers, were directly involved, with a wider audience reached 
through concerts, workshops, and educational activities. At the CCOM Node, we have actively integrated DigiScore into 
various aspects of our teaching, research, and artistic practice. Almost all undergraduate students and some postgraduate 
students specialising in electronic music participated in the rehearsals and performances of DigiScore case studies. 
DigiScore-related topics were also incorporated into compulsory courses at both Master's and doctoral levels, with 
practical exploration supported by our ZYLork Laptop Orchestra. In 2025, we took the opportunity of hosting TENOR 2025 
in Beijing to place digital scores at the centre of the conference's call for contributions. We successfully organised 
two concerts, bringing together works from different international DigiScore nodes and providing a platform for artistic 
exchange and collaboration. Beyond CCOM, we also extended DigiScore activities to Wuhan Conservatory of Music through 
lectures and workshops which attracted enthusiastic participation from local teachers and students. Through these activities, 
the CCOM Node has helped embed DigiScore in music education and creative practice, while extending its reach beyond our 
institution and strengthening connections within the international research community.

### Conferences

**[Presentation of Digital Score Solutions in the Context of Artificial Intelligence Technology: Performances and Workshops](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/_ZoyirNT8FjR1Y6xKoNnUQ)** 
20–21 June 2025 | Central Conservatory of Music (CCOM), Beijing, China. 
Organised by the Department of Music Artificial 
Intelligence and the CCOM DigiScore Lab, this two-day event formed part of an ongoing workshop series launched in 
late 2024. Under the direction of Prof. Li Xiaobing, with Prof. Kenneth Fields as workshop mentor, the initiative brought 
together composers, performers, researchers, students, and technology developers to explore innovative approaches to 
digital scores in the context of artificial intelligence. 

The event featured four case-study performances on 20 June, showcasing interactive music systems, AI-assisted composition, 
live electronics, experimental instruments, and networked music performance. Among these was Netronomia IV, developed by 
Kenneth Fields and collaborators and performed by ZYLork (ZhongYang Laptop Orchestra), demonstrating the creative 
possibilities of networked digital scores. 

On 21 June, a full-day workshop featured presentations, technical demonstrations, and discussions covering graphical and 
interactive scores, local network performance, AI-driven visual music creation, large language models, brain-computer 
interfaces, and experimental approaches to digital score systems. Participants also shared developments from the CCOM 
DigiScore Lab, including ZYLork's research and artistic practice, interdisciplinary collaborations, and the Chinese 
translation of Craig Vear's The Digital Score: Musicianship, Creativity and Innovation. 

The event concluded with an open discussion involving the project teams and participants. It provided an important 
opportunity to present and evaluate the outcomes of the CCOM DigiScore case studies, encourage interdisciplinary 
exchange, and further develop digital score research and practice within the institution. 


**[Tenor 2025 Beijing 2025.10 (3 days)](https://zhuanti.ccom.edu.cn/tenorbjen/info/1523/1201.htm)** 
25–27 October 2025 | Central Conservatory of Music (CCOM), Beijing, China 
The 10th International Conference on Technologies for Music Notation and Representation (TENOR 2025) was hosted by the 
Central Conservatory of Music in Beijing, marking the first time the conference was held in Asia. Organised under the 
leadership of Prof. Li Xiaobing, the conference provided an important international platform for advancing research and 
artistic practice in digital scores, music notation, artificial intelligence, and interdisciplinary music technologies. 

As the only Asian partner in the ERC DigiScore project, CCOM took the opportunity to strengthen the international visibility 
and impact of DigiScore research. The conference brought together researchers, composers, performers, and technology 
developers from around the world, including representatives from different DigiScore nodes, to share their research findings 
and creative practices. 

The programme included 33 academic papers, 16 artistic performances, and 3 workshops, alongside keynote presentations by 
leading international scholars and artists, including Craig Vear, Cat Hope, Georg Hajdu, and Pavlos Antoniadis. Two 
concerts provided opportunities to present innovative digital score works, including contributions from the international 
DigiScore research network, encouraging artistic exchange and collaboration across institutions and countries. 

Through TENOR 2025, the CCOM Node helped extend the reach of DigiScore beyond individual case studies and laboratory 
activities, connecting its research outcomes with a broader international community of scholars and practitioners. The 
conference also strengthened the relationship between academic research, music education, and artistic creation, 
contributing to the long-term development and dissemination of digital score practices in China and internationally. 

[Official conference website](https://zhuanti.ccom.edu.cn/tenorbjen/index.htm)
